# Voice

Built 2026-11-05 from two sent emails in `3_Library/Emails/`: a thank-you to an introducer (2026-05-13) and a call
recap to investors (2026-10-15).

## Tone
- Plain, warm and direct. Thanks people by name for the specific thing they did.
- To introducers: light and personal, with a small promise ("I owe you dinner").
- To investors: businesslike. Names who owns each next step and by when.

## Length and structure
- 60 to 140 words. Short paragraphs of one to three sentences.
- A recap uses a short bullet list, one owner and one date per bullet.

## Openings and closings
- Opens with the first name and a comma on its own line: "Parker," or "Riley, Quinn,".
- First line is a thank-you for something concrete.
- Signs off with "Damien" alone. No "Best", no signature block.

## Phrases he uses
- "Thank you for ..." / "Thanks for yesterday"
- "I owe you ..."
- "On timing."
- "If it slips, I'll tell you on <date>."
- Dates written as "21 October" or "Wednesday the 21st".

## Avoids
- Em dashes. Uses full stops and colons instead.
- Throat-clearing openers ("I hope this finds you well", "Just following up").
- Inflated words: "excited", "thrilled", "delighted", "crucial", "pivotal".
- "Not X but Y" contrasts and three-part lists for rhythm.
- Adverbs doing emphasis ("really", "truly").
- Hedged asks. He states the ask or the date plainly.

## Examples
- "Thank you for the intro to Avery. Three lines, and it still did the job."
- "I know a term sheet depends on the page. It comes on Wednesday the 21st. If it slips, I'll tell you on the 20th."

## Investor follow-ups to the labs
Checked: 2026-11-05

- **Who and goal:** deal leads at Anthropic, OpenAI and GV. Moves the round one step and keeps promises visible.
- **Length and structure:** 120 to 190 words. One opening line of thanks or context, then the substance, then the
  next steps. A recap uses bullets: one owner and one date each.
- **Opens:** first names only, on their own line ("Riley," or "Avery, Jordan,"). The first sentence thanks them for
  the call ("Thanks for yesterday.") or says why he is writing ("Avery said to ask in writing, so here it is.").
- **News:** names the lab's own event with its date and a link on its own line, then says in one sentence why it
  matters to FDE4Real ("That news matters to us." / "It matters because ...").
- **Promises:** states what he owes and the date. Adds what happens if it slips ("If it slips, I'll tell you on the
  20th.").
- **Closes:** with the next step or a direct question ("Avery, who should we ask?"). Signs "Damien".
- **Phrases that repeat:** "Thanks for yesterday", "Recap:", "On timing.", "It matters because", "I'd rather know."
- **Avoids:** em dashes; adverbs; "excited", "thrilled", "pivotal"; "not X but Y"; hedged asks ("I was wondering if
  perhaps"); closing pleasantries ("Looking forward to hearing from you"); bold or headings, except bold labels in
  a structured answer.
- **Example:** "On timing. I know a term sheet depends on the page. It comes on Wednesday the 21st. If it slips, I'll
  tell you on the 20th."
- **Rule from Damien:** "Name the date for anything I owe, and keep the news to one line."

## Thank-yous to introducers
Checked: 2026-11-05

- **Who and goal:** the person who made an intro. Thanks them and keeps them in the loop.
- **Length and structure:** 70 to 90 words, three short paragraphs.
- **Opens:** "Parker," then "Thank you for the intro to <name>." plus one line on why the intro worked ("Three
  lines, and it still did the job.").
- **Middle:** when the call is booked and how he will run it ("I will keep my part short").
- **Closes:** a small debt ("I owe you dinner." / "I owe you a coffee, and probably two.") and a promise to report
  back ("I will tell you how it goes."). Signs "Damien".
- **Avoids:** em dashes; gushing ("so grateful", "means the world"); asking for anything more.
- **Example:** "Your two lines did more than my deck would have."
